CONCEPT PROTOTYPE / GPU EXPERIMENT DECISION SYSTEM

Spend compute to learn.
Not just to run.

A concept for a human-in-the-loop preflight layer for expensive GPU experiments. The proposed system would combine the hypothesis, prior evidence, expected cost, available capacity, and decision value to recommend what to run, at what scale, and what result should unlock more compute.

01 / THE PROBLEM

GPU allocation is a
learning problem.

A run can be technically valid and still be the wrong next experiment. The real question is whether it buys enough decision-changing evidence to justify its compute, engineering time, queue delay, and opportunity cost.

02 / THE SYSTEM

A preflight layer before
expensive GPU runs.

The proposed product would turn an experiment request into an auditable recommendation. It would search what the organization already knows, estimate resource exposure, check constraints, and propose the smallest run that can change a real decision.

01

Experiment contract

Records the hypothesis, requested resources, decision to be changed, success threshold, stop condition, and owner.

02

Prior-evidence search

Retrieves similar runs, failed configurations, reusable results, and unresolved questions before new compute is approved.

03

Cost + duration estimate

Forecasts GPU-hours, runtime, queue impact, and budget exposure, then compares the request with actual historical runs.

04

Pilot designer

Finds a smaller proxy or staged run that can answer the same decision-relevant question before full capacity is committed.

05

Constraint check

Checks budget, cluster capacity, data readiness, evaluation dependencies, and conflicts with higher-priority work.

06

Human decision memo

Recommends approve, pilot, reuse, narrow, defer, or stop—with assumptions, evidence, limits, and a follow-on gate.

05 / RESEARCH BASE

The thesis has a
real academic foundation.

These papers motivate the product thesis: soft information, financing frictions, internal capital-market distortions, and staged investment under uncertainty. They do not validate this prototype, its illustrative rules, or any specific recommendation.

2002CORE THESIS

Information Production and Capital Allocation: Decentralized versus Hierarchical Firms

Jeremy C. Stein · Journal of Finance

Why it matters → when project knowledge is “soft” and difficult to credibly transmit, hierarchy can have trouble allocating capital based on that information.
2009/10R&D FINANCE

The Financing of R&D and Innovation

Bronwyn H. Hall & Josh Lerner · NBER / Handbook of the Economics of Innovation

Why it matters → R&D is unusually exposed to information asymmetry, intangible assets, uncertainty, and financing frictions.
2014INTERNAL CAPITAL

Firm Boundaries Matter: Evidence from Conglomerates and R&D Activity

Amit Seru · Journal of Financial Economics

Why it matters → evidence that organizational form and internal capital-market activity can affect the scale and novelty of innovation.
2004/05SHORT-TERM PRESSURE

The Economic Implications of Corporate Financial Reporting

John Graham, Campbell Harvey & Shiva Rajgopal · NBER / JAE

Why it matters → surveyed executives reported willingness to sacrifice long-term economic value to hit short-run reporting targets.
2000MISALLOCATION

The Dark Side of Internal Capital Markets

David Scharfstein & Jeremy Stein · Journal of Finance

Why it matters → internal politics and rent-seeking can distort capital budgeting, so the product must evaluate evidence rather than simply trust project advocates.
2025REAL OPTIONS

A Real Options Methodology for Multi-Stage Project Selection

Andrea Belz et al. · Annals of Operations Research

Why it matters → shows how staged R&D funding can be modeled as a portfolio optimization problem under uncertainty.
2026EXPERIMENTATION

R&D Uncertainty and Cycles

Nicolas Crouzet & Janice C. Eberly · NBER / AEA P&P

Why it matters → R&D can itself be valuable because investment generates information and resolves uncertainty.
2020RISK BIAS

The Risk of Caution: Evidence from an R&D Experiment

Carson, Graff Zivin, Louviere, Sadoff & Shrader · NBER

Why it matters → experimental evidence that decision-makers can favor safer R&D projects even when higher-risk projects offer larger expected payoffs.
06 / WHY GPU EXPERIMENTS FIRST

A TRACTABLE FIRST DECISION CLASS

Compute makes the cost of uncertainty visible.

GPU experiments recur frequently, compete for explicit capacity, and leave measurable outcomes. Smaller pilots can often precede expensive runs, while useful negative results can prevent another team from buying the same answer twice.

The product can prove that it improves experiment decisions here before expanding into a broader R&D capital-allocation system.

07 / MVP

Start narrow:
one tracker, one cluster.

The first question is deliberately specific: should this GPU experiment run now, at this scale, and under what limits? Launch in shadow mode—the system recommends and records, but blocks nothing.
